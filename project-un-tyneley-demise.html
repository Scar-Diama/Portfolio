The Project Brief

Used by the client today at Just In Tyne Escape, this project is a full escape room experience built by a team of 6 that I led as the project manager, lead programmer and director. I was responsible for the project plan, team management, technical implementation, task delegation and general direction of the project.

Constructed as a University Project, this escape room was designed around the client's needs and specifications - an escape room with a suitable theme for a family audience, an engaging narrative with a twist and two phases that would last a total of 11 weeks. Phase one would consist of the players going to smaller businesses around the local area to collect clues and information, which would then be used in phase two to solve the final puzzle and figure out the true killer. And depending on the player's performance, they would unlock 1 of 3 possible endings to the story, which would be revealed in a final cutscene at the end of the game.


Production Process

To deepen the understanding of the project and the client's needs, my deputy manager and I organised two in-person meetings at the Just In Tyne Escape office with the client to discuss the project in more detail. This included a two hour session discussing scope, requirements, the narrative, restrictions, ideas, and overall vision. After these meetings concluded, I organised a team meeting to discuss the project to inform the team of the client's needs and how we would achieve them.

Once the meetings had concluded, I created a project plan and timeline to ensure that the project would be completed ahead of schedule whilst also planning contingencies for any potential issues. This spreadsheet allowed me to delegate tasks to the team and track overall progress of the project.

Additionally it also allowed team members to raise any issues they had about their tasks and request assistance from other team members or myself. This was a key part of the project as it allowed me to ensure that the project was on track and that any issues were dealt with in a timely manner. Moreover, constant communication was maintained with the client to ensure that the project was progressing as expected and that any changes to the project were communicated and agreed upon.

Tasks were delegated to team members based on their strengths and weaknesses, as well as their availability. The tasks themselves were split up into smaller, manageable objectives that when all completed, would complete one section of the project. The tasks included (but not limited too): story boarding, puzzle brainstorming, puzzle design, puzzle implementation, feature ideas, clue designs and programming.

As for me, I delegated the harder tasks to myself. This includes: immersive videos, general character design, general programming, police report files, and final delivery to the client.

Final Product

Phase one and two of the project were completed. The team responded well to the project plan and the following were made:


  • Immersive Videos
  • Ten Created Characters and Case Files
  • Clues and Evidence
  • Interactive Puzzles
  • Custom Program
  • Ending VideoCutscene

The entirety of our plans for the project had been completed and the client was very satisfied with the results.
